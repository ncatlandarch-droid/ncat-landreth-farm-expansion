📐 Studio Ratio — Industry Standard (1:15)

I want to ensure we are all aligned on the 1:15 studio ratio standard. This is not simply an internal preference — it is the professional standard for landscape architecture education and a key metric in LAAB accreditation reviews:

  • LAAB Standard: The Landscape Architectural Accreditation Board evaluates studio class sizes as part of institutional capacity. Programs that consistently exceed 15:1 face scrutiny during accreditation visits regarding faculty workload, student access to instruction, and quality of design critique.
  • ASLA Best Practice: The American Society of Landscape Architects recognizes 15:1 as the standard for studio-based instruction, consistent with architecture, interior design, and other design disciplines.
  • Peer Comparison — NC State: NC State's landscape architecture program assigns multiple professors to a single studio section. They will soon offer an undergraduate program that will directly compete with ours. If we cannot match their faculty-to-student ratios, we will lose students to a program 90 miles away.
  • Utah State Example: Even at Utah State, where one professor oversees ~60 freshmen in studio, they achieve this by using graduate teaching assistants — each TA manages a sub-group of 15. We do not currently have a graduate program or TA pipeline to replicate this model.
Key Point Studio instruction is not a lecture. Each student requires individual design critique, project review, and hands-on guidance. A 1:15 ratio is not restrictive — it is the minimum required to deliver the quality of instruction that LAAB accreditation demands. If enrollment grows, we must add sections, not increase class size.
