1 Executive Summary
The Case in One Sentence The Program of Landscape Architecture at NC A&T has reached a scale, enrollment trajectory, and institutional significance that warrants elevation from a program within NRED to a stand-alone Department of Landscape Architecture within CAES.

NC A&T's LA program is the only LAAB-accredited undergraduate landscape architecture program at an HBCU in the United States. Despite this distinction, it operates as a sub-unit of the Department of Natural Resources & Environmental Design — sharing budget lines, faculty governance, and administrative infrastructure with forestry, geomatics, and environmental studies.

This structure creates friction: competing priorities for faculty lines, limited control over curriculum scheduling, diluted identity in recruitment, and an organizational mismatch with peer institutions where landscape architecture operates as an independent department or school.

4 Strategic Benefits of Elevation

🎯 Academic & Accreditation

  • LAAB alignment: Department-level self-study strengthens accreditation narrative
  • Curriculum agility: Own committee = faster response to industry changes
  • MLA pathway: Department status is a prerequisite for proposing a graduate program
  • Faculty tenure: LA-specific tenure criteria (Appendix G) governed by LA faculty
  • Studio ratio control: Direct management of 15:1 LAAB requirement

💰 Financial & Grants

  • Dedicated budget line: No competition with forestry/geomatics for resources
  • Grant eligibility: Many federal grants (USDA, NEA, EPA) require departmental-level applicants
  • Endowment potential: Alumni/industry donations directed to named department
  • Scholarship growth: Department-level fundraising vs. program-level requests

📣 Recruitment & Visibility

  • Clear identity: "Dept. of Landscape Architecture" vs. "NRED Program" on diplomas and transcripts
  • Website presence: Own departmental landing page, not buried under NRED
  • ASLA/CLARB visibility: Department status listed in national directories
  • Student recruitment: Prospective students search for "departments" — programs are harder to find
  • HBCU distinction: First HBCU with a Department of Landscape Architecture

👥 Faculty & Governance

  • Chair position: Direct report to Dean — voice at College leadership table
  • Hiring autonomy: Faculty search committees controlled by LA faculty
  • Workload management: Department-level decisions on teaching loads, releases, sabbaticals
  • Preeminence 2030: Aligns with NC A&T's strategic goal of elevating R1-caliber programs
