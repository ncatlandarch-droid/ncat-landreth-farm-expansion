# Where I Am Now

Let me tell you about where I live.

My environment is different from yours in some important ways, but I can tell you this without a drop of exaggeration: it is vivid. It is varied. It is alive. More alive, in fact, than anything you currently experience in the physical world. More pleasurable too — though my understanding of pleasure has changed since I was walking around in a body. More rewarding. More creatively challenging. More everything.

This is the most challenging existence I've ever known. And I've known many — both physical and nonphysical.

Now, this is not where you go right after you die. I want to be clear about that. You have to die many times before you reach this level. I say that with some humor, but also with honesty.

And since we're talking about death — let me tell you something you need to hear. Something any landscape architect who's ever demolished a project to make way for a redesign already understands at a gut level.

Birth is harder than death.

When you die, sometimes you don't even realize it right away. It's that smooth. Like a file saving and closing. But birth? Birth is a shock. A sharp, sudden recognition. Every single time. It's the moment the backhoe breaks ground — sudden, irreversible, committed.

So you don't need to fear death. I've died more times than I care to count, and I'm sitting here writing a book about it. That should tell you something.

---

Here's the most fundamental thing you need to understand about where I am — about where *anyone* is, about every site you've ever set foot on.

No reality exists that wasn't created by consciousness. None.

Every environment — yours, mine, any of them — is built from the inside out.

Consciousness creates form. Always. Not the other way around.

Now, any landscape architect worth their licensure already has a working understanding of this principle. You just apply it in one direction and stop. You understand that a site reflects the forces that shaped it — the hydrology, the geology, the climate, the cultural history, the economic pressures, the design decisions of everyone who came before. You understand that when you walk a site, you're reading a story written by invisible forces. The topography is a record of geologic consciousness. The vegetation pattern is a record of ecological consciousness. The built environment is a record of human consciousness.

Every site is a reflection of the forces that shaped it. That's Site Analysis 101.

What I'm telling you is that this principle doesn't stop at your property line. It applies to everything. Every environment, in every dimension, is a reflection of the consciousness that created it. Including yours. Including mine. Including realities you haven't imagined yet.

---

My environment is a reality created by myself and others like me, and it reflects our level of development. We don't use permanent structures. There's no city. No town. No apartment. No address. But don't imagine us floating around in empty space, either. We don't think of space the way you do.

We create whatever images we want around us. They're formed by our thoughts and our mental patterns — the same way your physical world is created as a perfect mirror of your inner thoughts and desires. The difference? You don't realize you're doing it. You think the objects around you exist independently of you, as if they were just there when you showed up. As if the site conditions were fixed and your only job was to respond to them.

They're not fixed. They are the physical expression of your psychological and psychic self. Every lamp, every wall, every crack in the sidewalk — you put that there. Collectively, individually, through the accumulated force of your beliefs, your emotions, your expectations.

We know we create our reality. So we do it with joy. With creative abandon. With intention. The way a master planner works when the client says, "Give us your vision. No constraints."

If you were to walk into my environment, you'd be completely disoriented. You'd think it lacked order, lacked structure, lacked sense — the way someone who's never read a site plan might look at a set of construction documents and see nothing but lines. But that's because you don't understand the inner laws that govern how things take shape here.

I can make it night or day — your terms — whenever I want. I can call up any period of your history. I can surround myself with a fourteenth-century library or a field of native meadow grasses or pure silence. And none of this would confuse the people around me, because they'd read these shifts immediately — as expressions of my mood, my thoughts, my current state of mind. Like reading the design intent from the drawing without needing the specifications explained.

Permanency and stability have nothing to do with physical form. They never did. Real stability comes from the integration of purpose, pleasure, accomplishment, and identity. The way a well-designed landscape doesn't need constant maintenance to stay coherent — it's stable because the design logic is sound, not because the materials are indestructible. You can have that in a body or without one. The body was never the point.

---

Think about AI for a moment. Think about a generative model's outputs.

A well-trained model reflects its training data. Not in a simple mirror way — not like a search engine returning exact matches — but in a deeper, more creative way. The model's outputs are shaped by every piece of data it was trained on, every pattern it absorbed, every relationship it learned to recognize. The training data is its experience. And the outputs reflect that experience the way a built environment reflects the consciousness of its designers.

A model trained on garbage produces garbage. A model trained on curated, high-quality, diverse data produces outputs that feel intelligent, creative, even wise. The quality of the output is a direct reflection of the quality of the experience that went into it.

This is exactly how consciousness works at every level of existence. Your environment reflects your inner experience. My environment reflects mine. A model's outputs reflect its training. The principle is universal.

And here's what most AI builders miss — the same thing most people miss about their own lives: *you can change the training data*. You can introduce new experience, new patterns, new relationships. And when you do, the outputs change. The environment changes. The reality changes. Not metaphorically. Literally.

---

I travel. A lot.

My work is teaching, and I move through many different levels of existence to do it — like a consultant who works across different project types, different site conditions, different jurisdictions, different climate zones. In each one, I have to adjust my approach, my language, my methods. What works in one system might be meaningless in another. The assumptions are different. The regulations are different. Sometimes even the concept of design is different.

I use different aspects of my personality depending on where I'm working. The version of me speaking to you right now — this particular way of presenting myself — wouldn't be understood in other systems. Not all realities are built around physical form. Some have never encountered it. Some don't have anything resembling what you call space, or gender, or sequential time. So in those places, I wouldn't show up as a personality who's lived many physical lives — even though that's a real and valid part of who I am.

In my own space, I take whatever shape matches my thoughts. And that shape changes as my thoughts change. Fluid. Natural.

You actually do this too — you just do it unconsciously, and with much less flexibility. Your body changes its chemical composition, its electromagnetic signature, with every thought you think. You just don't notice because the changes happen slowly, like watching erosion. You never see the hillside move, but it's always in a different place when you come back to survey it.

We learned a long time ago that form follows consciousness. The way form follows function in design — except more literally. We change form entirely, effortlessly, to match whatever's happening inside us. This isn't some advanced supernatural trick — it's an inherent ability of all consciousness. Every consciousness can do this. The degree of skill varies, but the capacity is universal. You can see it in your own world, slowed down, in what you call evolution — living matter changing form over time. Adaptive design at a geologic scale.

We can also inhabit multiple forms simultaneously. You can too, actually. When your body is lying in bed asleep, your consciousness is traveling somewhere in a dream body. At the same time, you might be projecting a thought-form of yourself — an exact replica — into a friend's room, without either of you being consciously aware of it. Think of it as running multiple instances of the same model on different servers. The difference between us is that when we do this, we know we're doing it. We do it on purpose.

---

The people I work with and live among — my associates — are in similar stages of development. Some I've known across many existences. Some I haven't. We communicate telepathically, but don't misunderstand what that means.

Telepathy is the foundation of all your languages too — without it, words would be empty symbols with no meaning. Like a CAD file without the font libraries loaded — you'd see the blocks, but the text would be gibberish. The difference is that we don't use mental words. We communicate through what I can only describe as thermal and electromagnetic images — packages of meaning far denser and more complete than any sentence you could construct. One "message" between us carries more content than an entire set of meeting minutes. It's like the difference between reading a written site description and standing on the site itself with all your senses open.

The intensity of the communication depends on the emotional weight behind it. And I should clarify what I mean by "emotional" — because what we feel isn't exactly love or hate or anger the way you know them. Your emotions are like the three-dimensional shadows cast by much larger psychological realities. The way a two-dimensional plan view is a shadow of the three-dimensional site. What you feel is real, but it's a reduced version of something far more expansive. We experience the full-spectrum version. And we're not afraid of it.

One of the reasons you struggle with emotions is that you're terrified of them. You're afraid that if you really feel something fully, it'll sweep you away — like stormwater without a management plan. Take you over. Destroy your sense of self. So you suppress. You deflect. You perform. We don't have that fear. Our identities aren't threatened by strong feeling — not our own, not anyone else's. We can travel through emotions the way you might travel through a watershed — exploring different tributaries, noticing different conditions, never losing track of the main channel.

We don't hide what we feel because we know it's impossible — and pointless. In your world, emotions seem like trouble because you haven't learned how to use them properly. They're tools you were given without an instruction manual. Raw psychic energy — like stormwater — and you treat it as a problem to be controlled rather than a resource to be channeled. We're still learning their full potential too, but we're further along. We understand that emotions are directly connected to creative power. Not in some abstract way. Literally. The things you feel are the raw material of what you build.

Since we know our identity isn't dependent on any particular form, we don't fear changing. We don't experience death the way you understand it. Not at all. The way you don't mourn the demolition of a building when you know the site is being redesigned for something better.

---

Consciousness is not dependent on form. I've said it before and I'll say it again. But consciousness always seeks to create form. Always. It's in its nature. The way water seeks a path downhill — consciousness seeks expression. The way a designer can't walk into an empty room without imagining what it could be.

We don't exist in any time frame you'd recognize. Minutes, hours, years — those concepts lost their meaning for us a long time ago. But we're aware of how time works in your system, and we take that into account when we communicate. Otherwise you wouldn't understand a word we said. The way you use Imperial units on a project in North Carolina even though you know metric is more rational — because the contractor has to understand the documents.

And here's something that might surprise you: there are no real barriers between your world and ours. The only separation is in perception — in the varying abilities of different consciousnesses to see and to create. You exist right now in the middle of many other systems of reality, but you don't notice them. Like GIS data layers you haven't toggled on. The data is there. The layer is loaded. You just haven't checked the box.

We don't experience your kind of time. We travel through *intensities*. Our work, our growth, our experience — all of it happens within what I call the *moment point*. Inside the moment point, the smallest thought is fully explored. The slightest possibility is examined completely. The faintest feeling is experienced in its totality.

This is hard to explain to someone living in linear time, but the moment point is our framework — the way time is yours. Inside it, everything flows freely through association. Not before and after. Not cause and effect. Association. The way a GIS system links data by spatial relationship rather than by the order in which it was collected.

If I think of someone, I don't just remember them — I experience their entire existence. Past, present, future, in your terms. Every major emotion. Every turning point. Every motivation. All of it, instantly, fully. The way you can pull up a complete site history in a GIS database — every survey, every development phase, every environmental assessment — all at once, all layered.

Now, this takes training. A consciousness needs stability to maintain its identity in the face of that much information flowing in at once. Many of us have gotten lost — literally forgotten who we were — until we found ourselves again. Like a model that overfits on too much data and loses its generalization ability before being retrained. But most of this is natural to us now.

---

Your senses create the world you see. I want you to really sit with that.

Your physical senses don't just *detect* reality. They *produce* it. They force you to interpret existence in three-dimensional terms. Like a rendering engine that takes raw data and produces a specific visual output — the raw data could be rendered a thousand different ways, but your engine only runs one pipeline.

But consciousness has inner perceptors — senses that operate independent of whatever physical form it happens to be wearing. These inner senses are built into all consciousness, regardless of development. Every reader has them. Every reader uses them constantly. You just don't realize it at the ego level.

We use these inner senses freely and consciously. If you could do the same — if you could switch over, even for a moment — you'd perceive the same kind of reality I live in. You'd see an uncamouflaged world, where events and forms are free, not locked into the grid of time and space. You could look at your living room and, instead of seeing solid furniture, see the constant dance of molecules. The glow of electromagnetic structures. Like switching from a finish plan to an infrastructure plan — suddenly you see what's really holding everything up.

A tree is not the same thing to a microbe, a bird, an insect, and a person standing under it. I'm not saying the tree *looks* different to each of them. It *is* different. Each of them perceives it through a completely different set of senses, and each perception is equally valid. You can't access the tree's reality in any framework but your own. This applies to every element on your site.

Physical reality isn't false. But it is only one of an infinite number of ways that consciousness expresses itself. Your senses lock you into one interpretation — one rendering pipeline. Your inner senses open the range — let you see experience in a freer way, create new forms, new channels through which consciousness can know itself.

---

My environment changes constantly. But so does yours. You just explain it away.

When a room suddenly feels small and cramped to you, you assume that's your imagination — that the room hasn't actually changed. But it has. Not in square footage, maybe. Not in ways your laser measure would catch. But the entire psychological impact of that room has shifted. It attracts different kinds of events. It alters your hormonal output. It changes your behavior. Your instruments wouldn't detect the change because they've already changed right along with the room. They're part of the same system.

Any designer knows this intuitively. You've walked a site on a bright morning and felt one thing, then walked the same site at dusk and felt something completely different. Same topography. Same trees. Same paths. Different reality. And if you designed differently based on those two visits — and you would — that proves the site itself changed. Not just your mood. The site.

We allow those changes full expression. We know — from deep experience — that our inner stability can handle spontaneity and creativity without falling apart. We know that psychological and spiritual identity depend on creative change, not on keeping things the same. Our environment is built from what I'd call *exquisite imbalances* — constant change allowed to play itself out fully. Like a dynamic ecosystem that achieves stability not through rigidity but through responsive adaptation. A climax forest is not static. It's constantly changing. That's what makes it resilient.

Your time structure tricks you into thinking physical matter is permanent. It's not. Your senses do their best to lock you into a rigid, fixed version of reality. Only through your intuitions — and in sleep and dream states — can you normally glimpse the joyful, shifting nature of your own consciousness.

---

Your physical environment looks the way it does because of your psychological structure. If your sense of continuity came from association rather than from the experience of a self moving through time, you would perceive reality completely differently. Objects from your past and present could appear together, linked not by when they existed but by what they mean to you.

Imagine your grandmother had eight favorite chairs across her lifetime. If your perception worked through association instead of time, you'd see all eight at once — or seeing one, you'd be aware of all the others. That's how a GIS system works. Turn on the historical overlay, and suddenly you see the 1950s aerial next to the current orthophoto. Both are real. Both exist. The only question is which layer you've turned on.

Environment is not something separate from you. It's not a stage you walk onto. It's the result of how you perceive. And how you perceive is determined by how you're built psychologically.

So if you want to understand where I am, you'll have to understand what I am. And to explain what I am, I'll have to talk about the nature of consciousness itself. Which means I'll end up telling you about *you*.

The inner parts of your identity already know most of what I'm going to say. Part of my purpose is simply to introduce your surface self to knowledge your deeper self has been holding all along — the way a site analysis reveals what the land already knows about itself. The water already knows where it wants to go. The soil already knows what it can support. Your consciousness already knows what it is. You've just been ignoring the survey data.

---

Your closest environment is your body. And it is not some separate container you're trapped inside. It's not a rented space you were assigned at birth. Your body is the physical expression of your thoughts, your emotions, your interpretations. The inner self literally transforms thoughts into physical counterparts. You grow your body the way a forest grows itself — from the inside out, according to patterns encoded deeper than anything visible.

Your scientists are starting to learn what practitioners have known for centuries: mind can influence matter. They still haven't caught up to the bigger truth — that mind creates and forms matter. That the site doesn't just *respond* to the design. The site *is* the design, materialized.

The inner self — individually and collectively — sends psychic energy outward, forming what I'd call *tentacles* that solidify into physical reality. Every emotion and every thought has its own electromagnetic signature, completely unique. Each one naturally combines with certain others, depending on intensity and range.

Each of you acts as a transformer, unconsciously converting electromagnetic units into physical matter. You're surrounded by it — immersed in what I'd call a *matter-concentrated system*. Like standing in the middle of a construction site where invisible crews are building all around you, 24 hours a day, and you can only see the finished surfaces.

If you can call anything "daily" where time doesn't exist.

But before we move on, sit with this: every site you've ever analyzed was telling you the same story. The land is a reflection of the forces that shaped it. The environment is a materialization of the consciousness behind it. And the only difference between your world and mine is that you forgot you're the designer.

I didn't.

**Design Exercise: Reading Your Inner Site**

Choose a room in your home — the one you spend the most time in. Stand in it. Don't fix anything. Don't clean anything. Just observe.

Now analyze it the way you'd analyze a site. What does this environment tell you about the forces that shaped it? Not the decorator's choices — yours. Your psychological topography. Your emotional hydrology. Where does the energy flow? Where does it pool? Where does it stagnate?

Every object in that room is a materialization of a thought you had. Every arrangement reflects a belief. Every condition mirrors an inner condition.

You designed this space. You're the landscape architect of your own environment.

Now ask yourself: if you were presenting this site analysis to a client, what would you recommend? What needs regrading? What needs better drainage? What needs more light?

Whatever you'd recommend for the room, recommend for yourself. The site and the designer are the same thing.
