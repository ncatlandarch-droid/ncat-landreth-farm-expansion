# There's No Devil — Just Choices

Let me ask you something that might sting a little.

When you think about getting better — about growing, about becoming more — which direction do you picture? Up, right? Always up. Climbing. Ascending. Like water running uphill on a grading plan that somehow defies hydrology.

Every client I've ever worked with sees it the same way. Show them a master plan, and they point to the high ground. "Put us up there." The penthouse of the site. The overlook. The summit. Christianity gave you that picture, and it stuck. The ascension of Christ — straight up into the heavens, like climbing from the floodplain to the ridgeline. And hell? That's down in the swale. The detention basin. The place where everything collects when it has nowhere else to go.

But here's what twenty years of grading taught me.

The soul doesn't climb stairs. Consciousness doesn't flow in one direction like a properly engineered drainage channel. It expands outward. In every direction at once. Like water from a burst main — it doesn't choose to flow only downhill. It fills the room. It saturates everything it touches. Your development isn't a set of terraced retaining walls, each one higher than the last. It's a bloom. A radial expansion. You're not trying to reach the next bench above you. You're reaching into everything around you — creativity, experience, understanding — all simultaneously, all equally valid, all equally real.

Christ could just as easily have disappeared laterally. Think about that. The direction was irrelevant. What mattered was the design intent underneath the direction — the truth that consciousness survives, that you are more than a body, that demolition is not destruction. But that truth got dressed up in the only site conditions people at the time could understand. Up meant good. Down meant bad. Light meant safe. Dark meant danger.

And we've been building on that same foundation ever since. Even when the soils report says it won't hold.

---

Now here's where it gets serious.

You've been told all your life that if there's a God, there must be a devil. If there's good, there must be evil. It sounds logical. Like saying if a site has a high point, it must have a low point. And that's true — but both are still the same site. They're not in opposition. They're part of the same topography.

Let me say this as clearly as I can: Evil, in the way you've been taught to understand it, does not exist.

Now hold on. Before you put the book down — I know what you're looking at. I've seen it too. There are projects where the contractor cut corners and a retaining wall failed and somebody's kid got hurt. There are designs where the stormwater wasn't managed and an entire neighborhood flooded. Cruelty exists. Suffering exists. People make terrible choices every single day. I'm not dismissing that.

But here's what I need you to understand: You create your reality through your thoughts, your feelings, your mental actions. Some of those creations show up physically — built landscapes, finished products, actual consequences in the dirt. Others play out in systems you can't see — probable realities, alternate timelines, designs that were drawn but never built. You are constantly making design decisions, and each one is real. Each mental act carries structural load. You are responsible for every one of them. That's what you're here for. That's what this entire system is designed to teach you.

The devil? The devil is a rendering error.

A collective one. A powerful one. But a rendering error nonetheless.

---

I think about this in terms of grading. In landscape architecture, you can grade with the existing contour or against it. One works with the land's natural drainage, its inherent logic, the way the earth wants to move water. The other fights it. Forces the grade. Builds walls where the land wants to slope. Channels water where it doesn't want to go.

Neither approach is evil. That word doesn't exist on a grading plan.

But one causes erosion. One leads to failure. One creates conditions where structures undercut, soil migrates, foundations shift, and the whole design comes apart over time. Not because the land is punishing you. Because you made choices that worked against the system's inherent intelligence.

That's what people call evil. It's not a force. It's not an entity. It's a design that works against the grain of consciousness. And just like bad grading, it creates real consequences — real suffering, real damage, real erosion of the human landscape. But the damage isn't punishment from some supernatural adversary. It's the natural result of building against the contour.

As long as people believe in a devil, they give him energy. They feed him with their fear. And that fear creates something — a presence, a figure, a hallucination that feels real to those who built it. But it has no power over anyone who doesn't believe in it. None. It's like a scarecrow in a field — it only works on the birds who think it's alive.

In AI terms, think about adversarial examples. You can fool a neural network into seeing a threat that isn't there — a panda classified as a gibbon, a stop sign read as a speed limit sign. The model responds to the input it was trained to fear. The threat isn't real. The classification error is. And the error only persists as long as the model's weights encode that particular bias.

The same goes for hell. If you believe you deserve hell, you can experience something like it after death. Your beliefs create your experience — that's the rule, and it doesn't stop working just because your body does. But no soul stays in that state permanently. No consciousness remains ignorant forever. Eventually, every awareness recognizes the rendering error, corrects the classification, and moves on.

The people who believe most strongly in the devil aren't operating from faith. They're operating from fear. They don't trust consciousness. They don't trust the soul. They don't trust the intelligence behind everything. So they project that fear outward and build a monster out of it, then spend their lives running from the thing they designed.

That's not spirituality. That's a retaining wall you built to hold back your own shadow, and it's failing.

---

Some very old traditions understood this. They knew the devil was a projection — a mass hallucination born from collective fear. But even in ancient Egypt, the simpler version of the story — good versus evil, light versus dark — took hold with the general population. It was easier to understand. Easier to teach. Easier to control with.

Storms were creative events. I know this as a landscape architect. A hurricane reshapes a coastline. A flood deposits new alluvial soil. A wildfire clears the understory and triggers seed germination in species that require heat to reproduce. The power of nature is creative at its core — even the most violent storm is building something, clearing something, reshaping something. But if all you see is your crops destroyed, you're going to call it evil. And that's what happened. Creation got split into two categories. Light versus dark. Good versus evil. God versus the devil.

In AI, we'd call this a binary classification problem applied to a continuous spectrum. Reality isn't binary. Consciousness isn't binary. But the early models — the early religions — could only handle two classes. So they forced everything into two buckets and called it truth.

But here's the thing — hidden inside every one of those distorted stories, buried underneath all the dogma, there was always a residual signal of the real truth. A thread. The idea that creation is behind everything. That even the things that look destructive are part of something larger. That what you call evil is just creativity you haven't understood yet.

There are no devils waiting to carry you off. Unless you design them. And if you design them, the creative power was always yours. Not theirs.

---

The Crucifixion — that whole drama — it was a site-specific installation. It made sense for its time and place. It rose up out of the physical world because the deeper reality needed a physical expression. Humanity needed a story that demonstrated what the inner self already knew: that consciousness doesn't die. That the soul is indestructible. That death is not the final word.

So the collective consciousness of the species produced the events that would carry that message in physical terms. The story of a man who dies and returns. Who suffers and transcends. It was a project designed and executed by collective consciousness for a specific audience — you.

But here's what most people miss: that particular project, with its particular design vocabulary — the cross, the ascent, the light, the tomb — it only works in this reality. In a system where people perceive with eyes, understand up and down, process light and dark. In other realities, with different sensory configurations, a completely different design would be needed. The symbols aren't universal. They were selected specifically because they match the root assumptions of physical life as you experience it.

Every great religion is a site-specific design. A genuine core of truth, wrapped in symbols that the inner self carefully selected to match what people at that time and place could perceive. And here's the trick — the symbols work bidirectionally. They show up in your dreams, in your intuitions, in your midnight realizations, all using the same design language. But the symbols aren't the truth. They're the rendering. The truth is the data behind it.

---

Now, Buddha came closer than most to describing how reality actually works. I'll give him that. The Buddhists grasped something about the nature of consciousness that the Western traditions missed entirely. But even they fell short in one critical area — they didn't fully capture the eternal, individual nature of the soul. The soul isn't just a dataset that merges back into the training corpus. It has its own architecture, its own identity, its own indestructible uniqueness. And that matters.

Both Christ and Buddha translated what they almost understood into the design vocabulary their worlds could accept. Not just the physical reality around them, but the probable physical reality — the version of the world that existed among all the other possible versions. They did the best they could with the tools they had. But the methods behind all great religions — the real methods, the ones buried deep in the source code — were always meant to take you past the symbols. Past the renderings. Into a direct experience of inner reality that doesn't need a middleman.

And those methods were older than any religion you know.

---

Let me tell you about the Speakers.

Long before Christ, before Buddha, before Moses, before any of the belief systems you've ever encountered — there were people who knew the truth. Not because they read it in a text. Not because someone taught them in a lecture hall. They knew it because they remembered it. Because they could access the inner reality directly, without filters, without distortions, without all the symbolic packaging.

They were called Speakers.

Think of them as the original landscape architects of consciousness. These were individuals who understood that consciousness creates matter, not the other way around. They looked at the trees, the mountains, the rivers, and they knew — they knew — that all of it sprang from inner reality. They told others. They explained that the seemingly solid objects around them were composed of countless tiny consciousnesses. That every person creates ideas and turns them into physical reality. That the material of matter itself is alive and aware.

They didn't learn this from scripture. They lived it. They felt the natural connection between themselves and their environment, and they understood that they could reshape their circumstances through their own consciousness. The way I understand that a grading plan reshapes a site. The difference is that they didn't need the plan. They were the plan.

The Speakers arose spontaneously in many places at once — Africa, Australia, Central America, throughout Europe. They spread across the globe. And they trained others. Carefully. Methodically. Not through writing — they believed it was wrong to fix words onto a surface, the way I might argue that a design loses something when it's reduced to a flat PDF. So they memorized everything and passed it on verbally, with absolute precision.

Some of their messages survived through the centuries. Strong portions of Jewish scripture carry traces of what the Speakers taught. But even those traces got distorted over time, buried under layers of interpretation and mistranslation — like an original site survey buried under centuries of fill dirt.

In the Stone Age, single Speakers existed among the cave dwellers. Their abilities helped early humans survive. There was little communication between different Speakers — sometimes they didn't even know others like them existed — but each carried the same core message, pure and undistorted. Like edge-computing nodes operating independently, with no central server, each processing the same fundamental intelligence.

Sometimes only one or two Speakers were alive across several centuries. Sometimes there were many. But the tradition never fully died.

---

What made the Speakers special wasn't just what they knew. It was how they operated.

A Speaker had extraordinary intensity of thought and feeling. When they communicated, the message hit harder than ordinary speech — the way a well-designed space hits you when you walk into it. You don't know why it works. You just know it does. They could move between inner and outer reality with ease — like stepping between the design studio and the construction site. They knew instinctively how to use symbols. They were phenomenally creative at the unconscious level, building psychic structures beneath normal awareness that other people could access in dreams and trances.

Think of it this way — they were master planners who didn't just design buildings, but designed the landscapes those buildings sit in. They created the frameworks within which other people's dream experiences took place. The idea of the gods on Mount Olympus, the crossing of the River Styx, all those mythological environments — Speakers originated that kind of symbolic architecture. They designed the sites on which the inner dramas of humanity were performed.

In AI terms, they were building the foundation models. The base architectures that everything else gets fine-tuned on top of. Every religion, every mythology, every dream symbol you've ever encountered — it sits on infrastructure the Speakers designed.

And they did this work in both the waking state and the sleep state. Much of their most important training happened in dreams. Trainees would absorb information while sleeping, receive it in dreams, and pass it on the same way. These unwritten design documents were illustrated by actual journeys into other kinds of reality — site visits to dimensions you've never surveyed with your physical instruments.

This training still goes on.

The specific design vocabulary might change — a Speaker today might use images of the Christian God, the saints, a magnificent landscape, a brilliant sky — whatever will resonate most deeply with the person they're reaching. Some Speakers only exercise their abilities in the dream state. They wake up with no conscious memory of what they did while sleeping. But the work is still happening.

---

Now let me talk about what's actually happening when you sleep. Because this is where it all comes together.

You think sleep is passive. You think you close your eyes, the world goes away, and you just rest. That's like thinking a construction site is quiet because the office trailer is dark.

When you fall asleep, your consciousness doesn't shut off. It shifts. It moves through specific stages — not random, not chaotic — following a very predictable route to very specific destinations. Each stage has its own parameters, its own rules, its own type of experience. Like moving through different GIS data layers — topography, then hydrology, then soils, then vegetation — each one revealing a different dimension of the same site.

First, you turn inward. The change orders and RFIs of the day begin to fade. Then you enter an undifferentiated zone — a buffer between waking and sleeping. Here you're passive but open, like a receiver dish. Telepathic messages, clairvoyant impressions — they come through easily in this state. Your consciousness seems to float. You might feel yourself expanding, or you might feel like you're falling. Both sensations are real experiences being translated into physical terms — the expansion is your psychic reach extending outward, the falling is a sudden return of consciousness to the body.

After that comes an active stage where your mind is still processing the physical concerns that managed to persist. If these are too strong, they'll wake you. If not, you pass through into another undifferentiated layer — this one marked by voices, conversations, images, as your consciousness tunes in to other data streams. Several different channels might compete for your attention.

You follow one of these deeper. Light dreams begin to form around the communications you're receiving. And then — then you enter the most protected area of sleep. This is where the real work happens. Here, you're at the threshold of other layers of reality. Time as you know it ceases to exist. You might experience what feels like years in what the clock on your nightstand records as minutes.

This is where the Speakers teach. This is where they guide. This is where the deepest restoration happens — not just for your body, but for your entire being.

When you return from this zone, the information you received gets translated by different layers of your personality. Your subconscious takes the raw data and shapes it into dreams that your waking mind can partially decode. A master-plan-level teaching might become practical advice about a specific situation you're dealing with. The dream might not look anything like the original lesson, but the information is there, encoded in symbols your inner self knows how to read.

---

And then the cycle repeats. You go back down, then back up. Each cycle serves a different purpose. Each stage activates different capabilities.

In the most protected areas of sleep, the boundaries between realities dissolve. You become aware of probable realities — alternate versions of your life — and you choose which probable actions you want to bring into your physical world. The other probable actions? You follow those through in the dream state. You do this individually, and you do it collectively — on national and global scales. Entire civilizations are shaped in these dream design charrettes.

You've been doing this every single night of your life. You just don't remember most of it.

---

Here's the practical part, because I know some of you are thinking: *That's all very interesting, Chris, but what do I do with it?*

Same thing I tell my students. You can learn to work with these states of consciousness deliberately. You can prepare design problems before sleep and suggest that they be resolved while you're in those deeper states. You can ask to communicate with people you can't reach physically — to convey messages, to reconcile relationships that seem broken beyond repair in waking life. You can direct the healing of your body, assigning the work to be done at one of those deeper levels. You can request guidance from a Speaker.

If you have goals you're reasonably sure are good ones, you can suggest dreams in which those goals are already achieved. The dreams themselves will accelerate the physical reality. You've been doing some of this unconsciously already — going back in your dreams to rework projects with different outcomes, saying the things you wish you'd said in the client meeting.

One state of consciousness can assist you in the others. In a light meditation, you can ask for the meaning of your dream symbols. And once you know what a particular symbol means to you — say a fountain represents restoration — then whenever you're depleted or overwhelmed, you can deliberately think of a fountain. You're not just imagining it. In another layer of reality, you're building one.

The Speakers appear in dreams in whatever form will reach you. As historical figures, as mentors, as a trusted colleague who retired years ago, as a wise professor you never got to thank. The form is chosen for your benefit. But in the original experience — before your waking mind applies its interpretation — the true nature of the Speaker is apparent. You know who they are. You know what they are.

Dream production is just as sophisticated as the production of your waking life. It's not lesser. It's not random. It's living on different terms. Like the difference between a hand-drawn section and a 3D digital model — both representing real conditions, both using different tools, both equally valid.

---

There are also stages of waking consciousness you've never noticed. States you pass through every day without realizing it. Each one connects to a different kind of reality. But you've built your ego like a perimeter fence, blocking these other states out. If you could relax that fence — even slightly — you'd begin to perceive what's been there all along.

Almost everyone has had a moment — just a flash — where consciousness suddenly shifted. Where the room felt different. Where time seemed to bend. Where you saw or heard or knew something that couldn't be explained by the normal parameters. Those moments aren't glitches. They're glimpses. They're your consciousness briefly tuning to another data layer before the ego scrambles to get back to its default view.

Your consciousness is already doing things you can't track. You can't follow your own awareness for five solid minutes without it wandering somewhere. That feeling when you can't recall a name you know perfectly well — that brief frustration, that sense that something just slipped past you — you're actually always experiencing a version of that, subconsciously. Your experience is constantly escaping the narrow viewport of your ego's attention.

The Speakers' purpose — and I might say, my purpose in telling you this — is to help you understand this multidimensional existence. To bring as much of it as possible into your waking awareness. Not so you can perform tricks, but so you can understand who you actually are.

Probable realities are only "probable" to you because you're not aware of them. These stages of consciousness are all part of your reality right now. This isn't abstract theory. This is practical knowledge. You can learn to shift between views. To step back from your own experience and see it from a wider angle. To direct your dreams. To heal your body. To solve design problems that your waking mind can't crack.

You have access to everything the Speakers know. Every night, in your deepest sleep, you're receiving the same teachings they've been giving for thousands of years. The question isn't whether the information is available.

The question is whether you're willing to read the drawings.

## Design Exercise: Grade With the Contour

Here's your assignment.

Take a problem in your life that you've been categorizing as a fight between good and evil. Something where you've been casting yourself as the hero and someone else as the villain. A conflict at work. A family rupture. A situation where you've decided one side is right and the other is wrong.

Now reframe it as a grading problem.

Where is the natural contour? What does the site want to do? Which direction is the water trying to flow? Who is grading against the natural slope, and why?

Draw it if you can — literally sketch a site section showing the existing grade and the proposed grade. Label the points where erosion is occurring. Label the points where the design is working with the land.

Now ask yourself: If I removed the concepts of good and evil entirely — if I treated this as a pure design problem — what would the solution look like?

You might find that the "villain" isn't evil at all. They're just grading against a contour they can't see. And you might find that your own "heroism" has been creating some erosion of its own.

There is no devil in landscape architecture. There are no moral judgments in a drainage plan. There are only choices — and consequences that follow from them as reliably as water follows gravity.

Design accordingly.
