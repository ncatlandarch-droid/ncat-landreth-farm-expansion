# Levels

Here's something nobody tells you about consciousness: it's already doing things you can't see.

Right now, sitting wherever you're sitting, your awareness is moving. Expanding and contracting. Zooming in on one thing, pulling back from another. You do this all day, every day, and you don't notice. Like a GIS operator toggling between data layers without thinking about it — topography, hydrology, soils, vegetation, infrastructure — each click revealing a different dimension of the same piece of ground.

Think about the last time you were deep in a design. I mean really in it — not pushing lines around on a screen but living inside the project. Seeing the site as it would be. Walking the paths before they were graded. Feeling the shade of trees you hadn't planted yet. For those minutes, the studio disappeared. The desk, the monitor, the phone buzzing with emails — gone. Not physically gone. But gone from your consciousness. Your awareness had moved to a different data layer, and the physical environment just faded to background.

Or think about a memory that hit you so hard you forgot where you were. You were back in that moment — the argument, the site walk where everything went wrong, the call from the hospital — and the present tense just dropped away.

You take this for granted. These shifts don't scare you. When you're lost in a design, you don't panic about the studio disappearing. When you snap out of a memory, you don't worry about finding your way back. You trust that reality will be there when you turn your attention back to it.

Good. That trust is important. Because those small shifts — those everyday fluctuations in your consciousness — they're training data for something much bigger.

---

Let me talk to you about layers.

In GIS, we call them data layers. Topography. Hydrology. Soils. Vegetation. Infrastructure. Zoning. Utilities. Each one overlaid on the same geographic coordinates. Each one real. Each one telling a different story about the same piece of ground. Turn on topography, and you see contour lines. Turn on hydrology, and you see watershed boundaries and flow paths. Turn on soils, and you see bearing capacity, permeability, pH. Same site. Completely different information.

Now expand that metaphor until it covers everything.

Everything you perceive is a data layer. Everything. The chair you're sitting in. The sky outside your window. Your own body. These are layers — physical representations of something deeper. You don't think of them that way because they're solid. Because you can touch them. But that's exactly what makes them the most powerful data layers of all. They're so convincing that you forget they're overlays and think they're the base map.

A real fire tells you that you're perceiving reality through your physical senses — the topography layer, if you will. Your body, your eyes, your nerve endings — all tuned to that channel. But a mental image of fire — one you see with your mind's eye — tells you something different. That's a signal that you've shifted to another data layer. And a fire you see mentally that gives warmth but doesn't burn? That's yet another layer entirely.

Symbols are the language consciousness uses to express feelings. Not words. Not grammar. Feelings. And as you move through different states of consciousness — from waking to daydreaming to deep sleep to something beyond all of those — the data layers change. They transform. They become more fluid, more intense, more direct. Until finally, at the deepest levels, the layers themselves dissolve and all that's left is pure experience. Pure knowing. No overlays. No legends. No metadata. Just the raw ground truth itself.

Most people never get there. But the path is available to everyone.

---

In a neural network, information passes through layers of abstraction. The first layer sees edges. The second sees shapes. The third sees patterns. The fourth sees objects. The fifth sees scenes. Each layer processes reality at a different level of complexity, and by the time you reach the deepest layers, the data has been transformed from raw pixels into abstract representations that bear no visual resemblance to the original input but contain far more meaning.

Consciousness works the same way. Let me walk you through it.

Start with a feeling. Let's say joy. Pure, genuine joy — the kind that hits you when you step onto a finished site for the first time, or when a design finally clicks after weeks of dead ends, or when your kid laughs and the whole world realigns.

In your normal waking state — the surface layer — that joy changes everything around you. The room looks different. Colors seem more saturated. Sounds seem cleaner. The world itself appears more vivid, more alive. And it's not an illusion — you're actually perceiving objects more clearly, rendering them more vividly in your consciousness. The environment seems to reinforce your joy, feeding it back to you, and that feedback loop lifts everything higher. But you're still dealing with physical objects. The chair. The window. The trees outside. The topography layer.

Now let that joy pull you into a reverie. A daydream. Eyes still open, maybe, but your mind starts to wander. Images come — memories, future projects, people, places. The joy stretches out through time, coloring the past and the future both. You're still using symbols, but now they're mental images rather than physical objects. They move faster. They connect more freely. They aren't bound by the rules of the physical layer anymore. You've toggled to hydrology — the flow of things, the movement, the connections between points that don't appear on the topo map.

Now imagine falling deeper — into a trance state, or into sleep itself. The joy generates images that might seem unrelated on the surface. Flying people. Playing animals. Landscapes of impossible beauty. Dream cities filled with light. None of it connects logically, but all of it is connected by that single feeling. Joy. The thread that ties every image together. You've moved past the standard data layers into something the GIS doesn't have a category for.

And all the while, your physical body is being healed. The beneficial feelings are literally renewing your body's recuperative abilities. This isn't metaphor. Your body responds to the state of your consciousness the way a living landscape responds to good soil conditions — growth happens when the medium supports it.

Go deeper still. The joyful images might become archetypal — Christ, Buddha, the prophets, figures of great spiritual power. These aren't random. They're symbols appropriate to this level of consciousness, representing the highest expressions of that same feeling. You've reached the deep layers of the network — the ones that see not edges or shapes but meanings.

And then — go even further. Past the images. Past the symbols. Past every representation you've ever known. Here, in this nearly unpopulated territory of consciousness, the overlays begin to flicker. They blink on and off. They grow faint, distant, and then they're gone.

And the soul stands alone with its own feeling. Stripped of every data layer. Every overlay. Every metadata tag. Just the raw, direct experience of joy itself — not the pictures of joy, not the memories of joy, not the words for joy, but joy as it actually is before your mind turns it into something else.

This is where the real design happens. In this place, the soul begins to explore the reality of that feeling in ways that can't be described in any language you know. Methods of perception and expression open up that would have been utterly incomprehensible at the surface layers.

And then, working the other direction, the soul takes that direct experience and pours it back outward — creating new symbols, new images, new living expressions — each one conscious, each one individual, each one expanding the range of what inner reality can become.

That's what consciousness does. It works in both directions simultaneously. Inward toward pure ground truth. Outward toward infinite expression. Forward propagation and backpropagation. And you are the consciousness doing it.

---

Your body is your most intimate symbol. Your most obvious data layer. And you carry the idea of a body with you through almost every state of consciousness. When you leave your physical body during an out-of-body experience, you don't float away as formless mist. You leave in another body — one slightly less physical. If you go further, you shed that one for another, even less physical. But the form remains. The idea of the body persists through your religious literature, through stories of the afterlife, through every tradition you know.

At some point — far beyond where most people travel — that symbol, too, falls away.

There was a time, speaking loosely, before symbols existed. A time so far removed from anything you'd recognize as reality that only in the deepest, most protected areas of sleep does any memory of it surface. To you, the idea of existence without data layers sounds like nonexistence. That's natural — you're so layer-dependent that a world without them seems impossible. Like trying to imagine a GIS with no data loaded. Just a blank viewport. But that era existed. And something was happening in it that was profoundly creative.

Even the states of consciousness that come after death still work with symbols, though with much greater freedom and understanding than you have now. But at the highest levels, symbols are no longer necessary. Creativity happens without them entirely. You can't grasp that state from where you are now — and that's fine. What you can do is start paying attention to how symbols appear in both your waking life and your dreams.

Because certain symbols will show up for you personally at certain levels of consciousness, and if you learn to recognize them, they become benchmarks. Survey markers. Reference points on a map of inner territory that most people never bother to chart.

---

Let me make this more concrete.

Say you're someone who regularly has lucid dreams or out-of-body experiences beginning from the dream state. You might notice that right before you leave your body, you always find yourself in a strange building — one you've never seen in waking life, but that feels oddly right. The building itself is a symbol. A survey marker. It tells you: *You've reached this particular coordinate of consciousness. You're ready to cross into the next state.*

Every person has their own version of this. Highly individual. Like the way every landscape architect develops their own graphic language on plan sheets — the same information communicated through different visual conventions. But unless you make an effort to explore your own inner landscape, these symbolic survey markers will flash by unrecognized. They'll seem like random dream noise rather than what they actually are — navigation instruments.

Some symbols stay with you for your entire life. Others change when you change — during periods of great transition, familiar symbols might transform, and that can be disorienting. The unconscious equivalent of your favorite reference file being reorganized. Same data, different structure, different feeling.

And this works in waking life too. If a dog represents joy and freedom to you, and then you witness a dog being killed, that symbol changes. Its emotional charge shifts. And the shift doesn't just affect your waking perceptions — it ripples into your dreams. The waking and the dreaming influence each other constantly. Like two data layers that update in real time — change one, and the other adjusts.

---

Here's the deeper point: all symbols represent inner realities. When you manipulate symbols — in your thoughts, your dreams, your creative work, your daily design decisions — you are manipulating inner realities. Every outward move you make is made simultaneously in the interior environment. In all the interior environments you're connected to. Symbols aren't just images in your head. They're charged elements — psychic elements — with their own characteristics of attraction and expansion.

When you rearrange the data layers in your mind, you're rearranging reality.

Physical objects follow one set of rules — they appear stable, permanent, solid. Dream objects follow different rules — they shift, transform, respond immediately to feeling. Both sets of symbols are equally real. They just operate under different conditions. The dream environment is as objective as the physical one. The objects within it are as faithful to dream life as physical objects are to waking life.

And the nature of the symbol — how it behaves, whether it holds still or transforms — tells you not just where you are emotionally, but which state of consciousness you've entered.

In a normal dream, objects seem stable enough. You walk through a building, it stays a building. But at another level of dreaming, a building might suddenly dissolve and be replaced by a landscape. A child might turn into a tree. The symbols are behaving differently because you've moved to a different processing layer, one where permanence isn't a ground rule and logical sequence doesn't apply. Feelings are expressed immediately, without waiting, without the time delay that physical reality requires.

In this faster state, you can draft an entire master plan. You can review your entire life blueprint. You can examine your past, your present, and your future — not as separate events stretched across a timeline, but as sections of a single interior site, all visible at once. Like looking at a GIS with every layer active simultaneously.

---

Now let me tell you something about what happens right at the edge of sleep — because this is where most people miss the richest data.

When you're resting, awake but with your eyes closed, images start appearing. Not the kind you choose to imagine — these come on their own. Some are recognizable: a tree, a face, a building. Others are just shapes, flowing one into the other in a kaleidoscope of changing forms. Most people dismiss these. They seem random. Disconnected from whatever you were just thinking about.

They're not random.

These images are your thoughts and feelings expressing themselves in symbol form the instant your eyes close. The symbols look disconnected from your thoughts because you don't recognize the translation. A fear about losing your project might show up as a deep excavation. A street scene from another century. A coffin. A dark wallet tumbling through the air. A winter landscape stripped bare. A character from a novel you read twenty years ago and forgot.

None of these images have anything obvious to do with your project. But they all carry the feeling of that fear. And mixed in between them might be counter-symbols — representations of hope. A spring garden. A loaded table. A new blueprint. Any sign of abundance that carries personal meaning for you.

The thought of your project never enters the picture. It seems like you've forgotten it. But underneath, your feelings are running deep — underground streams beneath the surface of your awareness — and those streams are generating every image you see.

If you lie still longer, the symbols change character. They might become less visual and more sensory — a distasteful smell, a feeling of subsidence, something unpleasant touching you. Each change signals a shift in your state of consciousness. A toggle between data layers.

And if you drift off to sleep from here, you'll generate two or three dreams that address the fear — dreams in which you try out possible solutions without the project situation ever appearing directly. The problem has been submitted to your deeper processing layers. In the most protected areas of sleep, the highest-level abstractions of your inner being take over. They see the situation with perfect clarity. They suggest a course of action — they don't force it, they suggest it — and they communicate that suggestion to your dreaming self.

Your dreaming self then creates another set of dreams — each one more specific, more translated toward waking understanding. The symbols narrow. They become more directly connected to your physical life. By the time you're close to waking, the dreams are speaking almost in code — code that your conscious mind can partially parse, even if you don't realize you're parsing it.

---

Here's a rule: The closer a symbol gets to your waking consciousness, the more narrow and specific it becomes. And the more specific it is, the less total meaning it can hold. Like a raster image at different resolutions — zoom in and you see individual pixels, sharp and distinct but containing almost no context. Zoom out and the whole picture appears, but the individual details blur.

The big symbols — the powerful ones that operate in the deep protected areas of sleep — they're the full-resolution dataset. They can contain vast amounts of information. They can decompose into dozens of seemingly unrelated dreams, serve as connecting threads between them, and still retain their original force while appearing in completely different forms at each level.

Your personal bank of symbols has been with you since the day you were born. And before. It contains the symbols of your past existences, added to and modified in each new life. This bank is activated — not simply stored. When you opened your eyes for the first time as a newborn, the internal visual patterns you already carried were activated by the physical act of seeing. You came into the world with inner reference files, and you kept adjusting your eyes until the exterior world matched the interior one.

This is critically important and completely misunderstood by science. The inner pattern comes first. The template precedes the terrain. If something is physically wrong with the eyes — if a person is born blind — that particular mechanism isn't activated. But if the person's reasons for being blind change, or if inner developments occur, the eyes can heal and the mechanism can activate later. The inner reality always comes first. The design intent always precedes the construction.

Those who are born into the same nationality in consecutive lives learn the language faster the second time around. Some infants think in the language of a previous life before they learn the new one. Like transfer learning in AI — knowledge from one training run accelerating performance on the next. All of this operates through the symbol system. The bank of inner symbols — images, sounds, sensations — is the foundation everything else is built on.

---

Sound itself is a symbol. You know that from a given point of silence, sound begins and grows louder. What you don't know is that from that same point, sounds also travel the other direction — deeper and deeper into silence — and those sounds are just as real, just as varied, and just as meaningful as the ones you hear. The thought you don't speak out loud has a "sound" that's inaudible to your ears but perfectly clear at another level of reality.

Trees have a sound. Stones have a sound. Your blood moving through your veins has a sound that isn't just the physical noise a stethoscope picks up — it's a symbolic sound that carries information about your entire being. Every material on a site has acoustic properties we measure. But I'm telling you there are acoustic properties we don't measure — can't measure, with current instruments — that are just as real.

In your deepest dreams, these sounds are automatically perceived and translated into visual images. They operate as a kind of compression algorithm. Given the right sounds, you could reconstruct your entire universe. Any single multidimensional symbol can contain all of the reality you know.

At different levels of consciousness, your senses separate and recombine. Smells have a visual quality. Sights can be perceived as sounds. The data layers come apart and merge in combinations your waking mind can't imagine. And each person has their own characteristic way of combining them — your own signature pattern of perception. Your own graphic language.

---

Now here's something that might rearrange your thinking: your thoughts don't just exist inside your head. They're apparent to other consciousnesses through telepathic communication. But beyond that — without your conscious knowledge — your thoughts also form what you might call phantom objects in other realities. Outside the range of physical matter as you perceive it. Your thoughts show up in other systems as objects — alive, vital, growing — the way plants grow up from seemingly bare soil.

In those other systems, your thoughts are the raw material. The native dataset. The building blocks of reality. Consciousnesses in those systems use your thoughts the way you use the physical environment — they work with them, build from them, perceive them with their own senses and take them as real. They have no idea those phenomena originated outside their system. Just as you have no idea that some of the "natural" elements of your own reality originated from elsewhere.

I'm not saying your physical world is made of discards from somewhere else. In your system, you directly participate in creating physical reality — your thoughts, feelings, and collective emotions become matter. Your system is more creatively direct than the ones I just described. But in those other systems, there's a different kind of creative innovation happening — a stronger group consciousness, a greater interchange between individuals, a deeper facility with symbols. Inhabitants of those systems recognize more clearly the connection between their creative images and their sensory data. They alter their environment deliberately. They edit the site plan in real time.

And there are constant bleed-throughs between systems. As you pass through various stages of consciousness — in sleep, in meditation, in those moments of waking reverie — you travel through areas that are native to other kinds of consciousness. You briefly operate on their data layers. You briefly perceive reality under their rules. Then you return, and the thread is lost. Your waking mind pretends there was never any gap at all.

---

Let me say something about fear, because this matters.

Symbols should be fluid. They should change and flow and transform freely — like a well-maintained GIS where layers can be toggled, reordered, restyled at will. When symbols become rigid — when they get locked in place and lose their ability to shift — fear is always the cause. Fear acts as a corrupt file at every level of consciousness. It hides the natural dimensions of symbols. It blocks the free flow of meaning. It turns symbols from tools of illumination into tools of deception — locked layers that trap experience rather than express it.

And here's something most people don't understand: symbols are aggressive. Not in a violent sense, but in the sense of forward momentum. The aggressive quality of symbols is what gives them mobility. Without that thrust, symbols would be static. They'd sit in a permanent environment and never change, like a GIS dataset that never gets updated. It's the aggressive, creative push of consciousness that propels symbols through different levels, that drives thoughts into realities you can't perceive, that keeps the entire system in motion.

Creativity and aggression aren't opposites. They're partners. They're both necessary for construction, and they're both present in demolition, though that's rarely understood. When they fall out of balance — when consciousness leans too far toward aggression or too far toward passivity — inertia sets in. The flow of symbols slows down or speeds up beyond what the psychological environment can handle.

And in those moments of imbalance, something strange happens. There's an almost inconceivable moment where a symbol gets caught between motion and no motion. A point of uncertainty. A flicker of non-reality. The symbol can be lost — dropped from an individual's experience, leaving gaps of inertia.

These gaps exist. They're real. You encounter them on many levels — psychologically, psychically, and even as blank areas in terms of space. You might find yourself in a state of consciousness where nothing appears. No landscape. No symbols. No signs of any consciousness besides your own. Just emptiness.

But these blank spots aren't dead zones. They're creative channels. They're open, passive, waiting. New symbols can be seeded into them. New creative ideas and innovations can be inserted through them. These are the spaces where genuine invention enters reality. Where something that has never existed before first appears.

Think of them as vacant sites. Not abandoned — available. Ready for development. Ready for something new to be designed and built on ground that has been cleared of everything that came before.

Future experience, in your terms, sits latent in these gaps. Symbols that haven't been activated yet float in them like seeds waiting for the right season.

The emptiness isn't the absence of design. It's design holding its breath.

## Design Exercise: Layer the Invisible

Here's your assignment. It requires nothing but a quiet room and twenty minutes.

Sit or lie down. Close your eyes. Don't try to meditate. Don't try to clear your mind. Just close your eyes and pay attention to what appears.

For the first five minutes, simply observe the images that arise. Don't judge them. Don't try to connect them to anything. Just watch. Note the shapes, the colors, the fragments of faces or landscapes or objects that float by.

After five minutes, choose one image — the one that felt most charged, most vivid, most insistent — and hold it. Don't force it. Just keep your attention on it gently, the way you'd keep your eye on a bird in a tree without startling it.

Now ask yourself: What layer is this? What data is it showing me? What feeling generated this image?

Follow the feeling, not the image. The image may change — let it. What stays constant is the feeling underneath it. That feeling is the ground truth. The image is just one possible overlay.

If you do this consistently — three or four times a week, for even ten minutes — you'll start to recognize your own symbolic survey markers. You'll learn your inner graphic language. And you'll begin to navigate the layers of consciousness the way you navigate a GIS — not by guessing, but by reading the data that was always there.

The layers are already loaded. You just need to turn them on.
