# The Other Versions of You

You know that moment — you're sitting at the desk, maybe studying a plan you've been grinding on for weeks, maybe staring at a site photo without really seeing it — and something shifts? The studio feels different. You feel different. Not dramatic, nothing you could explain to your project manager. But you're not quite in the same headspace you were a minute ago.

That's a doorway. And most designers walk right past it every single day.

I'm going to call that state A-1. It's one step away from your normal waking consciousness. One step. Not a giant leap. Not something that requires years of training or specialized equipment. It's right there, adjacent to the mind you're using right now, separated by the thinnest possible line.

Like the alternate site plan in your filing cabinet. The one you developed to the same level of detail as the built design, but the client went a different direction. That alternate plan doesn't stop existing because it wasn't selected. It's right there, one drawer over, fully resolved, fully real. You just aren't looking at it.

And in that state — quiet, alert, just slightly shifted — you can do things that your normal consciousness can't.

You can enter another person's experience and perceive what they need. You can examine your own body from the inside, seeing what's happening at the cellular level the way you'd read a soils report — not as abstract data, but as lived reality. You can manipulate matter from within — consciously, with full lucidity. You can step back from the pressures of your physical life and see your problems from a clearer vantage point, the way you see a site more clearly from the aerial photograph than from the ground walk. Solutions that seemed impossible might suddenly become obvious. Suggestions you give yourself — or that someone gives you — become dramatically more effective. Images form more easily and move more freely.

A-1 is a sidestep. A viewing platform beside your regular life where you can stand and see things with fresh eyes.

And it's simple to enter. You've been in it before. Many times. You just didn't know it had a name, or that you could use it deliberately.

---

Everyone has their own physical tells for this state. You might tap your fingers a certain way. You might stare slightly to the left or right. You might take a particular kind of breath. These little gestures are your body's way of saying: *We've shifted.* Once you learn to recognize them, you can use them as entry points — deliberately. You just have to notice. Then hold the state instead of letting it drift away. Then start exploring.

In A-1, your attention is still oriented toward the physical world, but you're perceiving it from a deeper layer. You can experience the present moment as it exists for your hand. For your spine. For any part of your body. You can feel the peace and the commotion that exist simultaneously inside your physical form — the quiet hum of organs working, the electric conversation between cells, the vast orchestration of biological life that's happening every second without your conscious participation.

There's something profound about this. A sense of unity with the living material you're made of. A wonder. An appreciation. Like standing on a site you designed and suddenly hearing it — really hearing it. The water moving through the bioswale. The insects in the meadow planting. The root systems pulling moisture from the amended soil. The entire landscape breathing, operating, living — and realizing you helped bring this into existence, but it has its own intelligence now.

With practice, you can become as aware of your internal physical environment as you normally are of the external one. And with more practice, the contents of your own mind become visible too. Your thoughts become as clear as your internal organs. You can see them. Symbolically, maybe — jumbled thoughts might appear as an overgrown site, which you simply clear and replant. You can request that your mental landscape be displayed as a site plan — a terrain of thoughts — and you can survey it. Edit it. Remove what's invasive and choking out the good growth. Replace it with something more intentionally designed.

I'm not saying it should be all ornamental garden. Balance doesn't mean perpetual manicured perfection. It means the landscape reflects a healthy inner ecology — varied, alive, with weather that changes but a ground plane that holds.

---

Now here's where it gets practical.

If you have a physical ailment, you can perceive it from A-1. You look inward, into the body, and you find it. You see it. Then you change what you see.

This might happen differently for different people. You might experience yourself entering the body the way you'd enter a building on a site walk — moving through corridors of your own biology, noting what's working and what's failing. Or you might become a point of light, moving through tissue and bone. Or you might simply be aware of the internal conditions without any visible form at all — pure data, like reading a soil boring log.

However it happens, you direct the body's energy where it needs to go. You bring together what needs joining. You adjust what's misaligned. You perform the equivalent of regrading a failed drainage area — redirecting flow, reestablishing slope, restoring function. You do what needs to be done — intuitively, naturally, using abilities that are already yours.

Then you shift your attention to the mental layer. You look at the thought patterns — yours, or the other person's — and you perceive them. They might appear as flashing text, spoken words you can hear, or as a landscape where thoughts take symbolic form. Like switching from the topography layer to the land use layer in your GIS. This shows you how the thoughts created the physical problem. Which ones were involved. How they connected.

Then you do the same with feelings. They might appear as bursts of color in motion — dark or light — or as a single overwhelming force. If the emotion is powerful enough, it might show itself in multiple forms simultaneously. The hydrology layer — everything that flows.

In all three layers — physical, mental, emotional — you identify what's connected to the problem and you remove it. With confidence. Not tentatively. Not hoping. With the same certainty you'd have marking a red line on a set of construction documents. This is wrong. Fix it.

Three adjustments. Three layers. Done from a state of consciousness that's one step from where you are right now.

---

A-1 isn't just for healing. It's a framework for creativity, concentration, study, rest, design thinking of every kind. You can think of it as a room — your room, designed by your own imagination. Or a landscape. Or a platform. Whatever image naturally comes to you when you enter this state, use it. It becomes your benchmark for this level of consciousness.

Now. A-1 can be used as a stepping stone in two different directions.

Deeper — into a more profound trance state, still connected to the physical reality you know.

Or sideways — into an adjacent level of consciousness, moving horizontally rather than vertically.

Let's go sideways first. Because this is where things get really interesting.

---

If you move one step sideways from A-1, you enter what I'll call A-1-a. This is no longer about deeper perception of the present moment. This is about alternate presents.

Alternate presents. Not past, not future — alternate versions of right now.

In my profession, we call them design alternatives. Every significant project generates them. The client says, "Show me three options." And you develop three fully resolved schemes — different grading strategies, different circulation patterns, different planting palettes — each one responding to the same site conditions but expressing a completely different design intent. Each one is real. Each one is buildable. Each one would create a different physical reality if constructed.

In A-1-a, you can try each choice.

Not in your imagination. Not as a thought experiment. You make the decision — mentally, fully — and then you perceive the reality that flows from it.

You enter that alternate present. You feel what happens to your body when you make that choice — does health improve? Does something feel wrong? You examine your mental state, your emotional state. Then you turn your attention outward and see the environment that results from the decision. Events appear. Consequences unfold. You can experience them vividly or simply observe.

Then you return to A-1. You rest. And you do it again with the second option. And the third. Each time, you're entering a different alternate present — a real one — and perceiving the real consequences that would follow.

When you return to normal waking consciousness, you make your decision based on actual experience, not guesswork. Not anxiety. Not the limited projection your worried mind throws together at 2 AM. You've been there. You've walked the site. You choose accordingly.

In AI terms, this is something like running multiple forward passes through a model with different inputs and observing the outputs. Except these aren't simulations. These probabilities are realities. Each decision you don't make still plays out somewhere. Like those alternate site plans in the filing cabinet — they don't cease to exist because the client selected Option B. In another probability, Option A got built. In another, Option C. You're not creating these alternate presents by visiting them — they already exist. You're just learning to open the drawer and look.

---

Beyond A-1-a, there's A-1-b — another adjacent level, two steps sideways from normal consciousness. This one deals with group realities. Mass probabilities. The movement of populations. The direction of civilizations. Like zooming out from the site plan to the regional plan — seeing not individual parcels but regional patterns, watershed-scale dynamics, infrastructure networks that connect entire metropolitan areas. A policy maker who could access this level would perceive the probable outcomes of decisions on a mass scale. It can also be used to probe probable pasts — to explore ancient landscapes and vanished settlements, as long as you're exploring the specific probable past in which they existed.

And beyond that, A-1-c — greater freedom of action and experience, some actual participation in the events you're perceiving. But these outer adjacent levels are increasingly divorced from your own reality, and for most people, A-1-a is as far as you need to go in that horizontal direction. It's the most practical and the most accessible.

---

Now let's go deeper. Directly beneath A-1, there's A-2.

A-2 is less physically oriented. You still have full lucidity, full awareness. Breathing slows. Temperature might drop slightly. Brain activity shifts to longer wavelengths. But the focus moves from the present condition to the past — specifically, your past as it exists within the probability system you know.

This is where you access past lives.

Think of it as archaeological survey. You're not inventing a past — you're excavating one. If you have a physical problem that can't be resolved from A-1 — if the source isn't in your current thoughts or feelings — you may need to go to A-2 and discover that it originated in another existence. A different body. A different century. A different set of site conditions that left an imprint deep enough in the foundation to affect the structure you're living in now.

In A-2, mental, physical, and feeling aspects of past personalities become perceptible. They'll appear in whatever way is natural to you — as images, as sensations, as knowing. You can find the origin of a design pattern, locate something that's been lost, trace a structural problem back to its source.

Beneath A-2: A-3. Here you encounter mass history. The movement of continents. The geologic ages. The knowledge of civilizations that inhabited this planet. Layers of sediment and mineral. The geological record — not as data in a textbook, but as lived reality. You're not reading the soils report. You are the soils report.

Beneath A-3: A-4. This is something else entirely. You're below the level of physical matter. Here, ideas and concepts exist in their raw state — electromagnetic identities that haven't yet materialized into physical reality. They're felt rather than seen. Perceived by parts of the brain that neuroscience hasn't figured out yet — those seemingly unused processing regions that no one can explain.

In AI terms, A-4 is the latent space. The mathematical space where the model stores its compressed understanding of everything it's ever learned, before generating any specific output. It's where innovation lives before it takes form. Complete inventions — designs that would change everything — exist in this reservoir, waiting to be decoded. Strong personal transformations happen here. The moments where everything shifts and you walk away from a life you thought was yours.

But — and this is important — any individual can pass through all of these levels and remain completely unaware. You can travel through them perceiving nothing, like driving through a city at night with your windows blacked out. The quality of what you experience depends on your intentions, your desires, your willingness to look.

The data is there. The layers exist. But they must be sought — through conscious desire or through deep unconscious need. If they're not, they remain unclaimed. Like a rich site that no one surveys because no one thought to look.

---

A-5 opens a dimension that most people never reach.

This is the level where any personality, from any time, any place, any probability system, can be contacted. Past, present, future — the divisions don't apply here. This is crystal-clear communication. Personalities meeting with full knowledge of their backgrounds, their histories, their identities — but also seeing those backgrounds as portions of a much larger design.

Messages flash between centuries at this level. Think about that. Leonardo knew of Picasso. The great designers, the visionaries — they've always operated here. They lived at this level of consciousness for much of their waking lives. Only the outermost shells of their personalities obeyed the dictates of their particular historical moment.

And it's not just the celebrated. Many people the world has ignored — whose work was lost, whose designs were never built, whose achievements were misunderstood — share in these communications. There's a simplicity required to reach this level, and that simplicity is as available to the humble as it is to the famous. Maybe more so.

There's an unending design charrette happening throughout the universe. Meaningful. Vital. People from your past and your future are involved in your present right now, at this level, discussing the projects humanity has faced and will face. This is the heart of creative collaboration. The place where it's all connected.

In AI terms, A-5 is the equivalent of a foundation model that has been trained on everything — every text, every image, every modality — and can generate responses that draw from the entire corpus of human experience. Except it's not a model. It's you. It's your consciousness operating at full bandwidth.

This level is usually reached in the deepest protected sleep, or in a sudden spontaneous trance. Great energy is generated there. And the information received has to be translated for your waking consciousness — otherwise, no physical memory survives the trip. But even when the ego remembers nothing, the experiences can completely reshape a life. Disastrous design decisions can be averted through illuminations you never consciously recall.

---

At any of these levels, you may encounter other phenomena — thought forms, energy patterns, projections from your personal subconscious or the collective unconscious. Some may look beautiful. Some may look threatening. Both responses say more about you than about the phenomenon.

Most of these manifestations are neutral. They have some energy of their own, but they need your attention and focus to become active. If something appears threatening, wish it peace and look away. It draws its power from your focus. Change your focus, and it deactivates. Like a rendering artifact that only appears when you zoom to a specific scale — change the zoom level, and it disappears.

But don't make the mistake of carrying your waking assumptions with you into these states. The rules are different. If you insist that everything must look like physical reality — if you insist on applying your default data layer to everything you perceive — you'll misinterpret everything you see.

---

Below these levels, there are more — and they begin to merge. In the next layer, communication becomes possible with forms of consciousness that have never been physical. Not in your past, not in your future, not ever. These are guardians. Custodians. Connected to your system of reality but operating entirely outside of physical form. Like the intelligence embedded in a well-designed ecosystem — the mycorrhizal network, the soil microbiome, the invisible infrastructure that makes the visible landscape possible.

Out-of-body experiences happen here, with the traveler finding themselves in environments of extraordinary beauty or grandeur — environments that come from their own mind, representing their deepest ideas about existence beyond the body. Speakers appear in whatever form will be most meaningful — as mentors, guides, old professors, whatever the person needs. And according to the individual's abilities and understanding, the communication can go very deep. Vast panoramas of past and future can unfold.

All of these levels are filled with layered communications that you can follow according to your purpose. Molecular structures send out their own messages. If you're not tuned to perceive them, they register as noise — meaningless static. But the information is there. It was always there.

---

Now let me talk about the horizontal layers — the adjacent levels that move outward rather than downward.

These involve alternate realities, each one further from your own. Many of these systems are so different from yours that you'd only accept any experience in them as fiction. In some, life and death as you know them don't exist. In some, time is experienced as weight. The root assumptions are so alien that your mind can't hold them in waking consciousness. Like trying to import a CAD file from a software platform that doesn't share any file format with yours — the data is real, but your system can't parse it.

You're less likely to travel in these directions deliberately. There are built-in barriers. Even projection from your universe into a universe of antimatter is theoretically possible from one of these adjacent states, but the electromagnetic makeup of your thoughts would be adversely affected.

Still — you visit these places in dreams. You fall into them spontaneously during sleep. In the morning, you remember a bizarre, fantastic dream and have no idea what it meant. That dream might have been a genuine perception of one of these adjacent realities. Not a metaphor. Not your brain shuffling files. An actual site visit to a different system of consciousness.

---

Here's the thing about alternate presents: they're not just alternate interpretations of one objective present. There are many alternate presents. You're focused in one of them. When your attention wavers — in a daydream, in a moment of distraction, in that half-second where your mind seems to blank — you might be glimpsing another.

The whole self, the soul, knows about its existence in all of these systems. You, as a part of it, are working toward the same state of awareness. When you become skilled, you won't be tossed randomly through states of consciousness every night. You'll direct the journey. You'll understand where you're going and why. The way a skilled landscape architect doesn't wander aimlessly across a site — they know which survey monument they're walking toward and what data they need to collect when they get there.

Consciousness is a tool that belongs to you. To the soul. You are not your consciousness — it's something you have, something you're learning to use. Like a designer learning software. You don't stop being yourself when you close the application. But the more you learn to use it, the more of yourself you can express through it.

Right now, you use consciousness in limited ways. You see physical reality and assume that's all there is. But that's just one active viewport. The other viewports are always running. They don't shut off because you're not paying attention.

---

The divisions I've been describing — A-1, A-2, the adjacent levels — these are arbitrary labels for something that's actually continuous. Like property lines drawn on a landscape. The terrain is all connected. You can cross from one area to another without any real boundary, because the levels all represent different attributes and directions within your own soul.

And traces of all of these levels are present even in your normal waking consciousness. Right now. This moment. You just don't notice because you've trained yourself to filter them out.

But sometimes — seemingly unrelated images rise into your mind. A flash of a face. A fragment of a scene. A feeling that doesn't belong to anything that just happened. Most people wave these away. Dismiss them. Move on.

Don't.

Those images are the beginning of a thread. If you acknowledge them, turn your attention to them, follow them — they'll lead you into at least two or three other layers of awareness. The images might change as you follow them, so that the original one looks nothing like the next. But the connection is there — intuitive, associative, creative.

A single image can suddenly open up into an entire mental landscape. But you'll never know that if you don't acknowledge the first survey flag.

---

What I'm describing is alternate focus. Turning your consciousness in a direction other than its habitual one, in order to perceive realities that exist simultaneously with your own. It's like learning to read a site plan instead of an aerial photograph — same data, different representation, different information revealed.

With practice, you can perceive different physical formations that have occupied a given space — past, present, and future. You can visit a location in a dream and see it as it was three hundred years ago and as it will be five years from now, all at once. To your waking mind, this seems impossible — you believe space can only hold one thing at a time, that one structure must be demolished to make room for another. But that's just how you perceive. It's not how reality works.

I think about the sites I've redesigned. The existing conditions survey shows me what's there now. The historical aerials show me what was there before. The design drawings show me what will be there after construction. Three versions of the same coordinates, all real, all accessible — and none of them canceling out the others. The old site still exists in the survey. The future site already exists in the drawings. The present site is just the version that happens to be physically expressed right now.

In alternate focus, you step aside from the root assumptions that normally guard and limit your perception. You step out of the moment as you know it, and you find you can return to it and it's still there. Consciousness only pretends to obey the rules of time. At deeper levels, it enjoys playing with time — mixing events from different centuries, finding harmonies between historical and personal moments, pulling them out of the timeline and examining them together.

You do this in your sleep. If you don't do it in your waking life, it's only because you've kept your consciousness on too short a leash.

---

Let me tell you about what your normal waking consciousness actually does, because you have some misconceptions about it.

It seems continuous to you. Seamless. Like an unbroken stream of awareness from the moment you wake up to the moment you fall asleep. But that's an illusion your ego maintains for its own comfort.

Your waking consciousness blinks off and on. Constantly. From fifteen to fifty times an hour, depending on what you're doing. It hops in and out of its normal focus, disappearing briefly into other modes of awareness, and then returning. And every time it returns, it pretends the gap never happened. It smooths over the interruption. Edits out the glitch. Creates the impression of seamless continuity.

In AI, we call this interpolation. The model fills in the gaps between data points to create the illusion of a continuous function. Your waking consciousness does the same thing — it interpolates between moments of actual awareness, creating the experience of unbroken continuity when the reality is more like a series of model checkpoints — saved states, with gaps between them.

During those tiny gaps, your attention is elsewhere. In mini-dreams. In associations. In intuitive processes that go beyond your normal range. You're perceiving other kinds of reality with other kinds of consciousness. Then you return, lose the thread, and your waking mind acts as though nothing happened.

Sometimes — very rarely — the experience during one of those gaps is so vivid, so intense, that it crosses the threshold and registers even in your waking awareness. Those are the moments when you catch yourself. When something strange flashes through and you think: *What was that?*

Those gaps are necessary. They're woven into the fabric of your awareness so seamlessly that they color your entire psychic atmosphere. Your waking consciousness floats on top of a vast, intricate web of other experiences — supported by them, nourished by them, dependent on them — while remaining almost entirely unaware of their existence.

---

You have memories of past lives. Complete ones. They're operating in your full memory system right now — alive, active, lit up like distant lights in a city seen from the air. Your dreams exist the same way — not archived and filed, but alive and running. Like model checkpoints that haven't been deleted. Every saved state of your consciousness is a version of you that still exists. Still operates. Still contributes to who you are now.

In those moments of conscious blank spots or fluctuations, these deeper memory systems are sometimes perceived. But your conscious mind refuses to accept them. It can't reconcile them with its own narrow training data.

When you accept that other realities exist — when you genuinely open yourself to the possibility that your consciousness can do more than what it's doing right now — something physical actually changes. Electromagnetic connections within your brain, your mind, your perceptive mechanisms shift. Reservoirs of energy come together. New pathways form. Your conscious mind doesn't lose anything — it gains. It becomes able to perceive what was always closed to it.

The fluctuations that seemed like threats — those brief blank spots that hinted at nonexistence — become something else entirely. Not evidence that you might disappear. But doorways to the source of everything you are.

Your future thoughts and actions are as real right now, in these deeper dimensions, as if they'd already happened. You are formed not only by your past but by your future. And by your alternate selves. These interactions are part of the architecture of your soul.

You can change your present reality from any of these levels. You can alter the direction of your life from a dream state, from a meditation, from a moment of deep creative absorption. Any layer of consciousness can serve as the active layer — the one through which you edit the design.

And just as you can look into other systems from here, beings in other systems can glimpse yours. From their vantage point, your physical world might look completely different. Your matter might seem impermanent. Your thoughts might have visible shapes. Your sense of time might be incomprehensible to them. They're surveyors too, trying to make sense of data they're unfamiliar with.

Your system is as probable to them as theirs is to you. Alternate focus works in both directions. The filing cabinet opens from both sides.

And when you realize that — really sit with it — you begin to understand that what you've been calling "reality" is just one set of construction documents for something so much larger that no single drawing set can contain it all. The soul doesn't ask you to abandon this set. It asks you to recognize it as one design among many.

And then it asks you to look wider.

## Design Exercise: Open the Filing Cabinet

Here's your assignment.

Choose a significant decision you're currently facing. Something with at least two clear options. Write each option on a separate sheet of paper.

Now sit quietly. Close your eyes. Let yourself settle into that slight shift I described — A-1. Don't force it. Just wait for the room to feel slightly different. You'll know when you're there.

Pick up the first sheet — mentally. Hold the first option in your mind. Not as a concept, but as a decision already made. You chose this. It's done. Now observe what happens. What does your body feel like? What images arise? What does the environment look like? Don't analyze. Just observe.

Come back. Rest. Then do the same with the second option.

When you're done, write down everything you perceived. Not what you think it means — what you saw, felt, heard, sensed.

You've just opened the filing cabinet. You've just looked at the alternate site plan. The one that exists whether you build it or not.

Now choose accordingly.
